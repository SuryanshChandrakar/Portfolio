Understanding Left and Right Brain Functions

Activities to Understand Left and Right Brain

Activity 1: Opposite Actions Game

This activity helps engage both hemispheres by requiring quick thinking and physical response contrary to verbal commands.

How to play: When the leader says "Stand up", participants must sit down. When they say "Sit down", participants must stand up. Other commands can include "Raise your left hand" (participants raise right hand), "Look up" (participants look down), etc.

Brain benefits: The left brain processes the verbal command while the right brain must override the instinct to follow the command directly, requiring coordination between both hemispheres.

Activity 2: Instant Word Usage

This exercise focuses on left-brain functions related to language and logical thinking.

How to play: A random word is given (e.g., "book", "computer", "water") and the participant must immediately describe its usage without hesitation.

Example: For "book" - "Books are used for reading, gaining knowledge, entertainment, and as reference materials."

Brain benefits: Strengthens the left brain's language centers and quick recall of factual information.

Activity 3: Word Association Chain

This activity engages right-brain creativity through free association.

How to play: A random word is given (e.g., "pen"). The first participant says a feature or associated word ("ink"). The next participant takes "ink" and says something associated with it ("blue"), and so on.

Example chains:

  • Pen → ink → blue → sky → clouds → rain → umbrella → protection → armor → knight
  • God → Mahadev → trident → ocean → waves → surf → beach → sand → castle

Brain benefits: Encourages right-brain pattern recognition, creative thinking, and making non-linear connections.

Self Introduction: Do's and Don'ts

Do's

  • Start with a warm greeting and smile
  • State your name clearly
  • Share relevant background information
  • Highlight key skills or interests
  • Maintain good posture and eye contact
  • Keep it concise (1-2 minutes typically)
  • End with a positive closing statement

Don'ts

  • Don't share overly personal information
  • Avoid speaking too fast or too slow
  • Don't memorize word-for-word (sounds robotic)
  • Avoid negative statements about yourself
  • Don't use filler words excessively ("um", "like")
  • Avoid reading directly from notes
  • Don't go significantly over time limits

Key Points for Effective Self Introduction

First Impression: You only get one chance to make a good first impression
Clarity: Speak clearly and at moderate pace
Relevance: Tailor content to your audience
Confidence: Believe in what you're saying
Authenticity: Be genuine, not exaggerated
Engagement: Show enthusiasm about yourself
